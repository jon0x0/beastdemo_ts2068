Building the landscape

Artwork adapted from the HTML5 Shadow of the Beast demo is resized and converted to 256×192 extended color with a custom Python encoder. A pink gradient sky and Sierra Lite dithering create apparent intermediate shades within the TS2068’s two-colors-per-8×1-pixel-cell limit.

Twelve scrolling bands—five clouds, one rock formation, five grass bands and one fence—move independently. Prepared pixel alignments and wrapping byte copies do most of the work; the rocks move one pixel at a time and cross the screen in about 10.7 seconds. Consistent rose/white palettes reduce blocky rock edges, while the six-pose runner is composed over its background in RAM before the finished rectangle reaches the display.

The reusable graphics and audio techniques are documented in the AISkill_TS2068 AI Skills library: extended-color conversion, gradient generation, transparency, parallax, buffered characters, native AY drivers, interrupt scheduling and emulator validation.
